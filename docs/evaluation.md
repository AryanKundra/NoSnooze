Usability Evaluation:

Method:
we evaluated NoSnooze using the SUS (Sytem Usability Score) test. In short its a test with 10 questions that produces a single usabiity score from 0-100. SUS was cosen as its quick to admisister and require no previous experience from participants while still providing a measurable way to see how accesiable your app is. 

Participants were asked to use the app on a an Android device for a short sesion which had them: set a new alarm with a label and choosing any repeat days, chaning the puzzle type, toggling the alarm off and back on, dimissing, and dismissing a test alarm by solving a puzzle of their choosing. After the session the participats completed the standard SUS questionnaire

Score calculation:
- Odd numbered items (postive) : contribution = Score - 1
- Even numbered items (negative): contribution = 5 - score
- sum all 10 contributions then multiply by 2.5 which gives us the score out of 100

Interpertation:
Our mean SUS score of 86.9 falls in the "Good" range approaching Excellent (above 90) indicating that participants found the app easy to use without needing assistance.

The high learnaiblity subscore of 93.8 reflects that all participants felt as though they could start using the app immediately without needing any prior instruction.

P3's lower score of 72.5 came from lower ratings on integration and consitency, during the session, P3 noted some initial confusion of what was suppsoed to happen after solving the puzzle, they werent sure if there was supposed to be another puzzle as they were expecting or if the alarm was fully stopped. This feedback prompted us to show a clear succuess state screen after finishing the puzzle.

Qualitative Observations:
During the session we noted the following

- what went well:
    - All four participants were able to successfully set an alarm and select their prefered type of puzzle with no guidance
    - The timer picker was immediately intuitive - partipantes compared it to the IOS clock app (which was the goal)
    - The repeat day selector was described as obvious/clean by 3 participants 
    - Two of the participants tried shaking the phone during the puzzle without being told by us

- Pain points/confusion:
    - I used my android phone to have them test it as everyone i know has an IOS phone and i wasn't sure if it would work on IOS but the phone was slow which was quite annoying for both participatns and us.
    - P3 was unsure if solving the puzzle fully silenced the alarm, this was addressed by adding a clear dismissed state
    - P1 initiallly looked for a snooze button before realising that there wasnt one (which is intended).
    - Two particiants didnt notice the swipe-down hint gesture until shown to them, we could be more clear on this

- Suggestions from participants:
    -P2 Sugessted adding a next alarm indication on the home screens so users are aware which alarm will fire next
    - P3 asked if it was possible for the triva puzzle to have categories e.g. science, history etc. 
    - P4 suggested a short vibration pattern if the answer entered is wrong.
